C. Proof of Theorem 3
Intuition: In causal (autoregressive) models, each position can only attend to previous positions. Therefore, later hidden states within
an n-gram window accumulate more context, and adding more context cannot reduce information about the target Y .
We first establish the necessary definitions, then prove the main result.
Definition C.1 (Mutual Information). For random variables A and B:
I(A; B) = H(B) −H(B | A)
(10)
Definition C.2 (Conditional Mutual Information). For random variables A, B, and C:
I(A; B | C) = H(B | C) −H(B | A, C)
(11)
Theorem C.3 (Routing Informativeness in Causal N-gram Windows). Let h1, h2, . . . , hn ∈Rd be hidden states within an
n-gram window under causal attention, and let Y denote the task objective. Then the routing informativeness is maximized
at the window’s final position:
I(Y ; hn) ≥I(Y ; hn−1) ≥· · · ≥I(Y ; h1)
(12)
Proof. Under causal attention, hidden state ht aggregates information from all preceding positions. Define the cumulative
context h1:t = (h1, h2, . . . , ht). By the data processing inequality, since ht is a deterministic function of h1:t:
I(Y ; ht) ≤I(Y ; h1:t)
(13)
It suffices to show that I(Y ; h1:t+1) ≥I(Y ; h1:t) for an arbitrary index t ∈{1, 2, . . . , n −1}.
By the chain rule for mutual information:
I(Y ; h1:t+1) = I(Y ; h1:t) + I(Y ; ht+1 | h1:t)
(14)
To verify this, we expand the right-hand side using Definitions 1 and 2:
I(Y ; h1:t) + I(Y ; ht+1 | h1:t)
=

H(Y ) −H(Y | h1:t)

+

H(Y | h1:t) −H(Y | h1:t, ht+1)

= H(Y ) −H(Y | h1:t, ht+1)
= I(Y ; h1:t+1)
(15)
Since conditional mutual information is non-negative (Cover, 1999):
I(Y ; ht+1 | h1:t) ≥0
(16)
it follows immediately that:
I(Y ; h1:t+1) ≥I(Y ; h1:t)
(17)
Since causal attention ensures ht encodes h1:t, we have I(Y ; ht) = I(Y ; h1:t). Applying this equality and the above
inequality iteratively for t = n −1, n −2, . . . , 1 completes the proof.
Implication for Routing: In causal models, the last hidden state hn of each n-gram window has access to all preceding positions via
causal attention, capturing the most task-relevant information. This justifies LiME’s last-token routing strategy for n-gram windowed
routing.
