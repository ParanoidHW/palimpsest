Online RL and entropy minimization.
Wang et al. (2021) first showed generalization
benefits of direct entropy minimization for image classifiers; modern studies demonstrate
similar effects in LMs trained with reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards Agarwal
et al. (2025); Shao et al. (2024). Kang et al. (2025) exploit the correlation between model
confidence and correctness for best-of-n sampling at test time, and Fu et al. (2025) leverage
it to re-weight rollouts during online and offline RL, observing consistent improvements.
We build on these results by designing our MTP objective and inference strategies around
the correspondence between low-entropy predictive distributions and generation quality in
reasoning-intensive domains.

Methodology
In Section 3.1 through Section 3.3 we motivate and define our novel MTP LM training
objective in an abstract manner and in Section 3.4 we discuss how to perform inference
using this kind of model. We also provide a formalization of how to perform a training
step using our method in Algorithm 1. Then in Section 4 we discuss specific aspects of our
method in more concrete, implementation focused terms with accompanying visualizations.
3.1
Next Token Prediction (NTP)
Let V be the set of V tokens in the vocabulary of our LM, and consider an input sequence X =
(x1, x2, ...xN) ∈V N. A transformer LM fθ(·) parametrized by neural network parameters θ
maps an input X into a sequence of logit vectors {ℓi ∈RV}:
fθ(X) = (ℓ1, ℓ2, ..ℓN) ∈RN×V.
To produce tokens from these logits, we consider a readout function g : R1×V →V which
could be:
g(ℓi) = argmaxj(ℓij)
or
g(ℓi) = sample(softmax(ℓi)).
For brevity, in subsequent sections we refer to the sample(softmax(·)) composition as just
“softmax” when contrasting it with “argmax”.
Let Y be the ground truth next tokens at each position in X such that yi = xi+1, and let the
predicted next token distribution parametrized by the model be
Pθ(xi+1|x1:i) := g(ℓi) = sample(softmax(ℓi)).
If the ground truth next tokens are represented as one-hot vectors denoted yi = {0, 1}|V|,
then the cross-entropy training objective for a next token prediction language model is given
as:

3.2
Multi-Token Prediction (MTP)
In our MTP model, the input x contains prefix tokens, followed by a block of k −1 MTP
“mask” tokens. On a forward pass, the model predicts k tokens, one at the final prefix
position and k −1 tokens at each masked position. We can define readouts that operate over
multiple logit vectors instead of one:
g(li:i+k) = argmaxj(ℓi:i+k,j)
or
g(li:i+k) = sample(softmax(ℓi:i+k)).
