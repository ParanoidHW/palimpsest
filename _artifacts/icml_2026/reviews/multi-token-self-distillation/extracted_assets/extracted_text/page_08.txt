5.2
Accuracy vs. Acceleration
In Figure 4 we summarize the performance of our L3.1-8B-Magpie based and Q3-4B-Inst-
2507 based MTP models after training on MetaMathQA. Results under the ConfAdapt scheme
are visualized on the same axes as the Static scheme by computing the average k value
that was chosen by the heuristic across all decoding steps over all the generations in the
evaluation and include its Std. Error in tables.
Under the Static scheme, as k increases the acceleration factor grows and the accuracy
decreases. Similarly, under the ConfAdapt scheme, as the confidence threshold τ drops, the
acceleration factor increases while accuracy decays, albeit more slowly. When compared
against Static k = 1 decoding, using the ConfAdapt scheme at threshold of 90% yields an
acceleration factor of more than 3× while decreasing accuracy for the L3.1-8B-Magpie by
less than 3%; the Q3-4B-Inst-2507 achieves 3× speeds at a slightly higher cost of a 7% drop
in accuracy. At more aggressive settings using more permissive confidence thresholds, the
acceleration factors are as high as 5× but cause a more significant reduction in accuracy.
5.3
Performance Across Tasks
In Appendix Tables 1 and 2, we present the same pair of models but evaluated over a larger
set of 6 evaluation tasks. We observe that even though both models were trained on only
MetaMathQA, transfer learning occurs. As the two initial models have different strengths
before MTP adaptation, both at step 0 and after training is complete, their performance
under Static k = 1 decoding varies considerably. However, we generally observe that larger
average acceleration factors occur on the GSM8K and BBH benchmarks, and that for the
more open-ended generative tasks like CNN DailyMail, we see lower acceleration factors
across all decoding strategies.
